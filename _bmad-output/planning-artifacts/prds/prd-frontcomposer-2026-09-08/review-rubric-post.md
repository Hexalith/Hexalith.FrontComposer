# PRD Quality Review — Hexalith.FrontComposer (post-reconciliation, 2026-09-08)

Reviewed: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prd.md` (updated 2026-09-08) and `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prd-addendum-2026-09-08.md`. Rubric: `.agents/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Delivery facts were not re-verified against the repository; this review judges the document.

## Overall verdict

The rewrite holds up. §0.1 is a real blocker list with pass/fail sentences, owners, evidence paths and dated state; §12 and §12.2 give every decision and open item an owner and an unblock condition; the FRs carry concrete IDs, numbers and wire shapes; and §1 states a dual thesis (safe-to-consume framework **and** trustworthy operator shell) that the SMs actually validate. What is at risk is narrower than last round: the one remaining decision with materially different engineering consequences (G-3: migrate to EventStore 3.103.0 or roll back to 3.91.1) is presented as an either/or with no recommendation or trade-off; the FC-NIP residual DW-679 (server-allocated keys never produce a fresh-row marker) is buried under SM-7 "met" instead of being stated as an operator-visible limit; and a dozen foreign identifiers (Epic 3/4 retries, AD-13/15/16, H1–H12/M-series, DW-679, E9-AI-n) are used without definition, which weakens standalone extraction. None of these is critical; two are high.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Strong on structure. §0.1 is the sole authority and says so ("This table is the only authority on what still blocks v1.0 publication"); each of G-1…G-6 has a falsifiable "What must be true" sentence, an owner and a named evidence artifact. §12's "Blocks" column links decisions to gates, §12.1 separates Closed / Routed / Accepted residuals, and addendum §6 (Rejected Alternatives) names what was given up on nine choices — the rubric's "trade-offs named" bar is met there. The frontmatter split (`product_approval` vs `v1_publication_authorized`) directly kills the previous "status launders release" failure.

Where it is weaker: the PRD is decisive about *what* is open but not about *which way* the last real fork should go. D-12 says "Publication requires either a dated migration approval to the built identity or a rollback" and stops. Rolling back is engineering work and re-tests; migrating is an external owner's approval. Those are materially different plans and the PRD carries no default, no recommendation, and no statement of what each path costs. Relatedly, §1 says "what remains before publication is the evidence and ownership closure in §0.1", which is true only if G-3 resolves by approval and G-6's Tenants boot succeeds first time; a rollback or a failed Tenants bootstrap is code work, and the Vision should not pre-decide that away. The PRD uses D-/OI- rows in place of `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts, which is a fair substitute for this shape, but two OI rows ("Default: accepted") are the rubric's "rhetorical open question" pattern.

### Findings
- **high** G-3 is the only remaining fork and the PRD does not take a side (§12 D-12, §0.1 G-3, §10 "Risk: EventStore identity drift") — "either a dated migration approval to the built identity or a rollback" is offered with no default, no owner-recommended path and no cost of each option, although rollback means code/Pact rework while migration means an external owner's signature. *Fix:* add a default to D-12 (e.g. "Default: seek migration approval to 3.103.0; rollback only if the EventStore maintainer declines by <condition>"), state what each path gives up, and mirror the default in G-3's State cell.
- **medium** Vision overstates how little remains (§1 last paragraph vs §0.1 G-3/G-6, §12.2 OI-3) — "what remains before publication is the evidence and ownership closure" ignores that G-3 may require a rollback, G-6 may fail its first attempt, and OI-3 is an unwritten enforcement story. *Fix:* "what remains is the closure in §0.1, most of it evidence and ownership; G-3 and G-6 can still require code work."
- **medium** The §0.1 authority rule contradicts the register it governs (§0.1 second sentence vs §12 D-1, D-2, D-3, D-5, D-8, D-10, D-13, D-14, D-15) — "Decision rows in §12 may not declare 'Blocks: None' while any row below is open", yet nine rows say exactly "None". §10 shows the intent was D-9 only ("D-9 cannot read 'Blocks: None' while §0.1 has open rows"). *Fix:* reword §0.1 to "D-9 may not declare 'Blocks: None'…" or rename the column value to "Blocks: no §0.1 gate".
- **medium** OI-3 is a missing security enforcement that is declared a non-gate without rationale (FR-19 consequences, §12 D-14 "OI-3 is a regression-baseline item", §12.2 OI-3 "not publication") — FR-19 says `AllowAll*` gates "must never be registered in a non-Development host", NFR-5 says MCP security "fail[s] closed", but nothing enforces it and the PRD does not say why documentation-only is acceptable for v1.0. *Fix:* one sentence in D-14 stating the accepted risk and the compensating control (e.g. sample host review, SM-4 audit assertion), or promote OI-3 to a gate.
- **low** Two open items are pre-answered (§12.2 OI-1 "Default: accepted", OI-4 "Default: accepted"; D-13 "None once confirmed") — these are confirmations, not open questions. *Fix:* move them to §12.1 "Accepted residuals / awaiting confirmation" and keep §12.2 for items whose answer is genuinely unknown.

## Substance over theater — strong

The content is earned. The Vision (§1) could not be swapped into another PRD: "domain teams should describe their operational surface once, in code, then get consistent human and AI access paths", with the specific mechanisms named (source generation, schema fingerprints, governance tests). NFRs carry product-specific bounds (NFR-1 `SDK 10.0.400`, NFR-8 `10_000` ms / `1_000` ms / `120_000` ms / `250` ms / `30_000` ms / 8 lanes, NFR-13 depth 1–2 graph). Risks in §10 are real and dated ("This PRD was stale by four weeks on 2026-09-08"). Addendum §6 is a genuine rejected-alternatives table with reasons.

Five primary users (§2.1) exceed the rubric's four-persona heuristic, but each one drives content: Adopter developer → UJ-1/FR-7, Operator → UJ-2/UJ-3/FR-11–FR-16, AI-agent integrator → UJ-4/FR-17–FR-19a, Framework maintainer → Table B verification owner, Release owner → G-1/FR-24. No finding. The one furniture-adjacent item is the counter-metric set.

### Findings
- **low** SM-C1 and SM-C3 are counter-metrics nobody was measuring (§9 Counter-metrics) — "Generated file count is not a success metric" and "CLI output volume is not a success metric" guard against pressures the PRD never exhibits; SM-C2 and SM-C4 do real work. *Fix:* drop C1/C3 or replace with a counter-metric that guards a real tension (e.g. "closing G-1 by ledgering tags without downloaded-byte verification does not count").

## Strategic coherence — strong

There is a thesis and the document bets on it twice: once as the product bet (§1 para 2) and once as the v1.0 definition (§1 para 3, "two things at once and this PRD commits to both"). The SMs validate the thesis rather than activity: SM-1 (a named adopter boots through three calls) validates "describe once"; SM-7 (fresh row only from own material command) and SM-8 (recovery after disconnect) validate "an operations shell an operator can trust"; SM-4 validates the fail-closed MCP path. Counter-metrics exist, and SM-C4 ("A green workflow is not release success") is the right one for a release-evidence PRD. §8.3 and §2.3 give a coherent platform-scope logic. Addendum §6 records that "Restate v1.0 as a developer-framework-only release and drop operator freshness" was considered and rejected — the PRD knows what it chose not to be.

Minor: nothing measures the "same descriptors produce both the human UI and the MCP surface" half of the thesis (parity), and two metrics (SM-3, SM-6) lack the "State:" line the rest carry.

### Findings
- **low** SM-3 and SM-6 have no current-state line (§9) — every other SM records "State: met / unmet / pending"; these two do not, so a reader cannot tell whether drift visibility and UX governance are currently green. *Fix:* add "State:" with the evidence lane name and last-observed date.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs would let an engineer write a test. FR-2 names diagnostic IDs per failure (HFC1009, HFC1006, HFC1005); FR-4 gives exact density thresholds; FR-12 and FR-15 give millisecond budgets and say configuration changes "require focused `FakeTimeProvider` evidence"; FR-19 replaces prose with a request-class → public-shape table; FR-30 names the exception type, the SignalR group key and the storage-key format. §0.1's pass/fail sentences are also testable (G-6: "renders at least one generated projection and one generated command, with a dated evidence artifact").

The gaps are a small number of adjectives that survived, a performance NFR that points at unnamed files, and a status-map overlap that will confuse story extraction.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-1, FR-2, FR-7, FR-8 appear both as ship blockers and as delivered regression baselines (§5.0 Table A row 3 "FR-7, FR-8, FR-1, FR-2 (via SM-1)" vs Table B row 1 "FR-1 to FR-12") — §0.1 says anything not gated "is a regression baseline", yet these four are in both tables. The FRs are delivered; what G-6 gates is *adoption evidence* (SM-1), not the FRs. An engineer reading Table A will reopen delivered work. *Fix:* remove the FRs from Table A and add a row "SM-1 (adoption proof for FR-1/2/7/8) — G-6", or retitle Table A "Requirements or metrics whose evidence is gated".
- **medium** "Urgency ordering" is load-bearing but undefined (FR-10 consequence "Home directory supports … urgency ordering"; UJ-2 "sees Modules ordered by urgency") — nothing in the PRD, glossary or addendum says what makes one Module more urgent than another (badge count? status severity? recency?). *Fix:* one sentence defining the ordering key and tie-break, or a pointer to the exact `ux-design.md` rule.
- **medium** NFR-9 has no bounds of its own (§6 NFR-9) — "must stay inside the checked-in benchmark thresholds and cache caps" names one cap (`MaxProjectionFallbackPollingLanes=8`) and otherwise defers to unnamed files. The rubric asks for bounds, not adjectives; a pointer is acceptable only if the file is named. *Fix:* name the benchmark threshold file(s) and quote the two or three headline budgets (palette scoring, generated render) inline.
- **low** Residual vagueness in tooling FRs (FR-11 "Column filters are debounced" — no interval; FR-14 "retryable pre-accept failures" — no class list; FR-20 "Paths are sanitized when needed"; FR-21 "refuses unsafe paths"; FR-6 "deterministic and bounded" — bounded by what). *Fix:* give the debounce value, enumerate retryable failure classes (transport timeout, 5xx, …), and replace "when needed" / "unsafe" with the rule or a pointer to the CLI contract that defines it.
- **low** NFR-8 restates FR-12 with one extra bound (§6 NFR-8 "`10` s closed-restart timeout" is absent from FR-12, which says its bounds are "restated in NFR-8"). *Fix:* add the closed-restart timeout to FR-12 or drop the "restated" claim.

## Scope honesty — adequate

Omissions are mostly explicit. §2.3 Non-Users and §8.3 Out Of Scope do real work (author signing "deliberately dropped, REL-5"; container images; no-code CRUD). §13 indexes four assumptions, retires two with dispositions, and the two live ones (A3, A4) say what they were inferred from and who must confirm. §12.1 "Accepted residuals" names OI-2, DW-679 and the 9.8 deferred items. Open-items density (6 gates, 8 OIs, 2 live assumptions, D-9 re-approval) is moderate for a readiness PRD and every item has an owner and an unblock condition, so it is not a blocker.

The one place the reader is left to infer is operator-facing: the FC-NIP residual DW-679 is listed as a deferred-work ID, but its consequence — that a command whose row key is allocated by the server can never produce a fresh-row marker under the pre-dispatch-capture rule in FR-13 — is never stated, while UJ-2 promises the indicator "on rows his own recent commands materially changed" and SM-7 is marked met.

### Findings
- **high** DW-679's operator-visible consequence is hidden behind a ticket ID (FR-26 "Residuals: DW-679 (server-allocated keys need a typed post-dispatch identity proof)"; addendum §3; vs UJ-2, SM-7 "State: met", FR-13 "unknown identity … suppresses publication") — under FR-13's rule, create-style commands with server-allocated keys resolve to unknown identity and are silently suppressed, so the fresh-row promise in UJ-2 does not hold for that command class. The PRD never says so in words an adopter or operator would recognise. *Fix:* add an explicit `[NON-GOAL for v1.0]` line under FR-13 or §8.3 ("Fresh-row markers are not produced for commands whose target key is allocated server-side; tracked as DW-679"), and qualify SM-7's "met" with that exclusion.
- **medium** FR-25 and A4 disagree on what "latest published" means (FR-25 "the compatibility gate baseline currently tracks the latest published release (`4.3.0`)" vs §13 A4 "tags v4.2.0, v4.3.0, and v4.4.0 correspond to published NuGet packages"; addendum §1 dates v4.4.0 to 2026-09-07) — if v4.4.0 is published, the baseline is one release behind and the PRD should say so; if it is not, A4 is wrong and G-1's scope changes. *Fix:* state which it is in FR-25 and reconcile A4/G-1 accordingly.
- **low** "approved fallbacks remain" for `<AuditTimeline>` / `<ConsequencePreview>` (§8.3) names no fallback and no approval record. *Fix:* name the fallback component/behaviour and the decision that approved it.

## Downstream usability — adequate

The Glossary (§3) is present and is used: Module vs Bounded Context, Module Tab, Projection Flyout, Command Target Identity, Opaque Failure Token and Publication Authorization are each defined once and then used with the same spelling in FRs, UJs and SMs. FR IDs are contiguous (FR-1…FR-30 plus FR-19a and FR-29.1–29.7); UJ-1…UJ-6 each have a named protagonist carrying context inline; every §-reference and D-/OI-/G- reference resolves. Sections mostly stand alone.

What does not extract cleanly is the set of identifiers imported from other artifacts without definition. A story writer pulling FR-15 alone cannot know what "zero Epic 3 retries, and exactly one Epic 4 transient retry" means; FR-29 rows cite "H2, M1", "M-series" from a review not linked; G-2 and §12.1 cite "AD-13/AD-15" and "AD-16"; G-5 cites "E9-AI-1…E9-AI-6"; FR-26 cites "DW-679". The glossary covers REL-*/GOV-1/BUILD-REL-1 but not these families.

### Findings
- **medium** Foreign identifier families used without definition or pointer (FR-15 "Epic 3 retries" / "Epic 4 transient retry"; FR-29 table "H2, M1", "H11, H12, M-series"; §0.1 G-2 and §12.1 "AD-13/AD-15", "AD-16"; §0.1 G-5 and §8.2 "E9-AI-1…E9-AI-6"; FR-26 / D-4 "DW-679") — none is in the Glossary and none links to the owning artifact, so the sections that use them cannot be pulled out alone. *Fix:* add one Glossary line per family ("H1–H12 / M-series — finding IDs of the 2026-07-04 architecture quality review at <path>"; "AD-n — GOV-1 spine architecture decisions at <path>"; "DW-n — `deferred-work.md` entries"; "E9-AI-n — Epic 9 sprint action items in `sprint-status.yaml`") and rewrite FR-15 to say what the two retry classes are rather than which epic introduced them.
- **low** SM numbering does not read in order (§9: Primary lists SM-1, SM-2, SM-2a, SM-3, SM-4, SM-7, SM-8, SM-9; Secondary lists SM-5, SM-6) — IDs are unique and stable, but a reader scanning for SM-5 looks in the wrong list, and "SM-2a" is the only lettered ID. Acceptable if stability for `epics.md` is the reason (addendum §6 gives that reason for FRs, not SMs). *Fix:* either state that SM IDs are frozen for cross-reference or renumber once.

## Shape fit — strong

This is a brownfield, chain-top technical-capability PRD for a developer framework with a real operator surface, and it is shaped like one. UJs are few (six), each with a named protagonist, and they are used where the operator experience carries product weight (UJ-2, UJ-3) and where the developer experience is the product (UJ-1, UJ-5, UJ-6); there is no UJ theater for the Release owner, whose work is correctly expressed as gates and FR-24. Existing versus new is distinguished at the program level (§5.0 Tables A/B/C, §8.1 baseline, §8.2 status), which is the right granularity when nearly everything is delivered. §11's package table with TFM, baseline mechanism and breaking-change policy per package is exactly what a brownfield framework PRD needs and what the previous version lacked. The heavy program-management content (§0.1, §5.0, §8.2, §12) is unusual for a PRD but is justified by the document's stated purpose ("consolidates the delivered product baseline, the completed post-MVP remediation program, and the remaining publication gates").

One small shape note: all six UJs describe delivered behaviour, but the PRD does not say so, and the rubric asks brownfield PRDs to mark existing versus new journeys.

### Findings
- **low** UJs are not labelled as existing behaviour (§2.4) — every journey is delivered baseline, but a downstream UX or story workflow cannot tell that from the section itself. *Fix:* one lead sentence in §2.4: "All six journeys describe delivered v1.0 baseline behaviour; no new journey is added by this PRD."

"Degraded" is used both as a Command Lifecycle terminal-degraded state (FR-15) and as an adjective for a projection circuit (FR-12 "never permanently degrades", FR-29.2 "never stays degraded"); harmless but worth a glossary note.
