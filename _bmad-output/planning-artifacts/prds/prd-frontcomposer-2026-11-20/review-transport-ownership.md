# Transport ownership and adopter behavior

The canonical PRD and September addendum were checked against FrontComposer main at `aa016201`, its README, the approved migration plan, CLI behavior, and the November 20 audit correction. Two retirement requirements would misdirect work. Two acceptance details affect what adopters can verify.

- **[high]** FR-20 and FR-21 plan retirement of a retained compiler tool (§0 opening at line 12, §1 line 55, §5.6 lines 427–442, §11 line 675) — The PRD calls the CLI an “obsolete compatibility tool” and requires McpCli contracts before cutover. The merged plan explicitly retains local `inspect` and `migrate`, keeps them outside agent exposure, and gives their safety and continuity obligations to FrontComposer. A team following the PRD could schedule a replacement transport or treat retirement as a delivery goal. Evidence is in `README.md:30` and `references/Hexalith.AI.Tools/hexalith-mcpcli-migration-instructions.md:33`. *Fix:* Mark the September decision as superseded for these two commands. Carry local support and its build, freshness, write-safety, and privacy limits through the vision, both FRs, and the package table.
- **[high]** The MCP host retirement rule omits its Projects dependency (§5.5 line 372, §11 line 673) — “Retire after approved Hexalith.McpCli replacement or withdrawal evidence” allows a reader to plan host removal once FrontComposer capabilities have dispositions. The approved gate also requires coordinated retirement with the Projects plug-in after all host consumers are accounted for. Removing the host separately could strand a dependent plug-in. Evidence is in `README.md:31` and `references/Hexalith.AI.Tools/hexalith-mcpcli-migration-instructions.md:117–125`. *Fix:* Link the host requirement and package row to the coupled retirement gate, including its consumer inventory, sequence, and live/release/rollback evidence.
- **[medium]** Migration failure semantics leave the scope of atomicity unclear (FR-21 line 446) — “Apply mode is atomic” lets adopters assume that a failed run leaves all source files untouched. Each replacement is atomic. Earlier writes survive a later failure, and cancellation can interrupt the report. The documented result affects recovery after a partially applied migration. Evidence is in `docs/reference/cli.md:54–59` and `src/Hexalith.FrontComposer.Cli/MigrationApplier.cs:28–46`. *Fix:* Specify per-file atomicity and path/hash revalidation. Include a two-file failure case covering the first file’s persisted edit and the exit/report behavior of the later failure.
- **[medium]** Packaged inspection lacks a representative acceptance case (FR-20 lines 433–438) — The consequences check formatting, filtering, and redaction. Those checks could pass when the installed CLI finds zero compiler-emitted files. The November 20 audit documents that exact failure and its correction. Its evidence includes five forms and one grid on Counter, with stable JSON across repeated runs. See `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/sprint-change-proposal-2026-11-20.md:19` and `:41`, and `tests/Hexalith.FrontComposer.Cli.Tests/InspectCommandTests.cs:213`. *Fix:* Reference the packaged-tool regression as acceptance evidence. Require a known command and projection to appear from compiler output, retain flat-layout compatibility, and exclude files from unrelated generators.
